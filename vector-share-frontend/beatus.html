Frequently asked questions about the St. Beatus Caves

The St. Beatus Caves are one of the largest stalactite caves in Switzerland, located on Lake Thun in the Bernese Oberland between Thun and Interlaken. The cave system has been explored over 14 kilometres, one kilometre of which is secured for visitors and accessible all year round. In addition to the cave tour, the St. Beatus Caves also offer a museum, a restaurant, a nature park and numerous events.

The St. Beatus Caves are located on the northern shore of Lake Thun in the Bernese Oberland, between Thun and Interlaken. The site is located directly on the Way of St James and can be reached by car, public transport and boat. The nearest towns are Sundlauenen and Interlaken.

Adults pay CHF 20, children (6-16 years) CHF 12 and the family ticket costs CHF 52. Admission to the cave museum is included in the price. Dogs are welcome for CHF 10.

Yes, the caves are open all year round. The temperature in the stalactite cave is a constant 8-10 °C - regardless of the season. Warm clothing and good footwear are recommended.

According to legend, Beatus was an Irish messenger of the faith who came to the cave on Lake Thun in the 2nd century. He is said to have driven a dragon out of the cave and spent the rest of his life there. The legend turned the cave into a place of pilgrimage and gave it the name by which it is still known today.

The cave tour is suitable for children. Pushchairs are not permitted. In addition to the cave tour, there is also the Flimbo Trail (interactive adventure trail for children), a playground with a dragon slide, a public fire pit and children's menus in the Stein & Sein restaurant - everything you need for a successful family outing.

Ready for your cave experience?
